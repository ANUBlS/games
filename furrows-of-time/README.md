# Furrows of Time

A first-person farming game in the browser. You start in the year 300 with bare hands on a field of 65,536 plots and farm your way through twelve ages to the year 3000.

## How it plays

- Walk into the grass. Your tool plows every plot it reaches. Pass again to sow, and come back when the crop is ripe to harvest it.
- The mode bar above the Work button picks what your tool does: **All** does whichever step each plot needs, while **Plow**, **Sow** and **Harvest** do only that one step. Plow also plows growing and ripe crops back under, and those crops are lost.
- Plowing and sowing pay nothing. Harvested crops go into your bag.
- When the bag is full, walk to one of the four red barns at the edges of the field and empty it, or hand it to one of your workers standing within 4 metres of you, who carries it to the barn. Selling the bag is the only way you earn coins yourself. The **Empty bag** setting at the bottom of the shop chooses **Automatically** (the default: the bag empties by itself as soon as you are at a barn or next to a worker) or **By hand** (tap the button).
- Spend coins in the shop on upgrades: tool reach, swing speed, walking speed, harvest value, growth speed, bag size and rock tools.
- About 1,070 rocks lie in clusters across the field: stones, rocks, big rocks and boulders. Nothing can be sown on a rock. To clear one, switch to Plow mode with a rock tool strong enough for its size; each rock costs coins to remove. Rock tools run from a crowbar in 300 to a matter dissolver in 3000, with a new one and a new price in every age, and later tools make each rock cheaper to clear.
- When you have enough coins, buy your way into the next age. Each age brings new crops, new tools and a new look.
- Some ages also set work you must finish during that age before you can leave it: plots plowed, and set amounts of particular crops harvested. Only work you do yourself counts; your workers' plowing and harvests do not. The age panel counts finished tasks, and the shop shows each one with a progress bar.

| Leaving | Also needed |
|---|---|
| 800 | plow 2,000 plots, harvest 2,000 barley |
| 1200 | harvest 5,000 cabbage and 1,000 barley |
| 1600 | plow 12,000 plots, harvest 3,000 potato and 8,000 tomato |
| 1850 | harvest 20,000 cotton |
| 1990 | plow 70,000 plots, harvest 60,000 sunflower and 10,000 corn |
| 2000 | harvest 100,000 soybean |
| 2026 | plow 150,000 plots, harvest 150,000 strawberry |
| 2500 | plow 300,000 plots, harvest 300,000 moonberry and 20,000 glow rice |

The other ages (300, 1940 and 2100) only ask for coins.

## Seasons and weather

- The year turns through spring, summer, autumn and winter, 4 minutes each. Every crop can only be sown in certain seasons (wheat in spring, autumn or winter, tomato and cotton only in summer). In winter, crops that are not winter-hardy stop growing until spring. The seeds list greys out what cannot be sown now.
- Weather changes every minute or so. Rain makes crops grow 1.5 times faster, but now and then washes out a small patch. A storm marks an area with an orange ring, and 18 seconds later floods it. Crops under the water die and the ground goes back to grass, so it must be plowed, sown and harvested again. While the water stands, nobody can walk into it and nothing can be worked there; a flood drains after about 30 seconds, a washout after about 14. Replowing flooded ground does not count toward an age's plowing task. Winter brings snow, which covers the land.
- Autumn turns the trees orange, and snow turns the field white.

## Fertilizer

From 1850 on, many crops need fertilizer to sow: cotton and corn 1 unit per plot, sunflower and strawberry 2, moonberry 3. Soybean needs none. Buy fertilizer in the shop; its price follows the age you are in.

## Farm location

When you start, pick where your farm is: America, Russia, Azerbaijan, China, India, Brazil, France or Egypt. Each pays more for the crops it is known for (corn in America, wheat in Russia, cotton in Azerbaijan and Egypt, and so on). You can move later, for a fee.

## Workers

Hire up to nine workers in the shop. Each has a trait: hard workers move 1.5 times as fast, steady ones at normal pace, slow ones at 0.6, and idlers now and then stop working. Give each a job (All, Plow, Sow or Harvest); they walk to the nearest plot that needs it, use your seed and fertilizer, and bring their harvests straight to your coins. Wages are paid every minute and differ from worker to worker; if you cannot pay, everyone stops until you can.

When a worker stops, **Encourage** them (free, works about two times in three) or pay a **Bonus** (always works). People sleep at night, can fall ill in rain, snow or winter (and may die of it in the cold), can be hurt by tools or lightning, and sometimes quarrel. From 2026 on, robots take their places: they work day and night and never fall ill, but lightning can knock them out for a minute.

## Day and night

A day lasts three minutes, about two of them daylight. The sun climbs and sets, nights are dark with stars, and you carry a lantern.

## Ground and snow

After a harvest the ground rests before it can be sown again; you can plow it straight away. The rest is about half the crop's growing time. Rain leaves the ground wet and shiny, with drops splashing around you. Snow settles on everything, and snowy ground cannot be plowed or sown without a snow tool from the shop: a snow shovel (300) for you, a snow plough (1850) for you and your workers, and heated fields (2026), which also keep every crop growing through winter.

## Cheat codes

Type these in the box at the bottom of the shop: MONEYBAGS (coins for the next age), NEXTAGE (go straight to the next age), HARVESTMOON (ripen every crop), FERTILE (10,000 fertilizer), SUNSHINE, STORMY, SNOWFALL, NEXTSEASON, DAYBREAK, NIGHTFALL, FULLCREW (nine hard workers, free), CLEARROCKS, QUICKSALE (sell your bag where you stand).

## Field art

The shop sells field-art commissions: Alien, Person, Flying saucer, Star, Heart and Rocket. Pay to start one where you stand; a red flag marks the middle. Grow the figure in crops around the flag, north at the top of the map. Each square of the figure is one block of plots, bigger with bigger tools. The figure counts as done when 90% of its squares are right. A commission pays five times its price, or ten times if you finish with no hints. Hints: **Corner stakes** mark the figure's corners, and **Chalk outline** draws every square on the ground and on the map.

## More crops

Every age has two more crops besides its main one: a quick, cheap crop and a slow, valuable one. Oats and flax (300), rye and fava beans (800), onion and carrot (1200), pepper and pumpkin (1600), sugar beet and peanut (1850), rice and sorghum (1940), lettuce and canola (1990), quinoa and blueberry (2000), kale and saffron (2026), sky melon and coral bean (2100), sunfruit and nebula corn (2500), crystal wheat and void lily (3000).

## Gold

About one rock in thirty holds a gold nugget. Which rocks is hidden until you clear them, and a nugget pays far more than clearing the rock costs. Cleared rocks are gone, but every 45 seconds or so a few new rocks come up out of empty grass, often near you, and some of those hold gold too.

## Controls

- **Phone:** left thumb walks, drag anywhere else to look. The Work button turns the tool on and off.
- **Computer:** W A S D to walk, drag with the mouse to look, Space turns work on and off, E empties the bag at a barn, 1 to 4 pick All, Plow, Sow or Harvest.

Progress is saved in your browser.

## Sound

Plowing, sowing, harvesting, clearing rocks, emptying the bag and entering a new age each have their own sound. Music is generated as you play in four styles: Lute, Pipes, Piano and Synth. **Match the age** moves from lute in antiquity to synth in the future. Both are set at the bottom of the shop. Sound starts after your first tap or key press.

## Graphics

Models are built from rounded shapes: people with faces, hands, boots and straw hats; oxen and horses with rounded bodies, tapered legs, horns or manes and swishing tails; tractors with treaded rubber tyres, curved mudguards, a grille and headlights; barns of red planks with white trim, an X-braced door and shingled roofs; and a post-and-rail fence round the field. The farm is drawn like a haystack game: big haystacks built from thousands of separate straws stand by the barns and around the farm, loose straw lies on plowed soil, straw flies up when you harvest or plow, and dust drifts in the sunlight. The field also has real-time shadows from the sun, a sky with sun or moon and drifting clouds, distant hills, and grass, crops and trees that sway in the wind. The shop has a Graphics setting:

- **High:** sharp shadows and the densest grass. The default on computers.
- **Medium:** softer shadows and less grass. The default on phones.
- **Low:** no shadows and no grass, for slower phones.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a current browser. It is a single file and loads three.js r128 from cdnjs, so it needs an internet connection.

To serve it locally:

```
npx serve .
```

## Code

- `src/logic.js`: game rules (field, crops, upgrades, ages, bag, saving), with no DOM or 3D code.
- `src/page.html`: the page, HUD, shop and 3D world (three.js), with a `/*LOGIC*/` marker where the rules go.
- `index.html`: the built game. Rebuild it after editing `src/` with `node tools/build.js`.
- `tools/tune.js`: sets the price of every age by simulating a player. Edit `TARGET` (minutes per age), then run `node tools/tune.js src/logic.js` and rebuild.
